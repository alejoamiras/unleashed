AQuiet broadcast

What it says
The lab is a channel, and it is on air. This is the app's own look at page scale, so the jump from landing to app is invisible.
Background
2 px dots on the 8×16 character cell. A signal scanline steps down one row every 100 ms with a three-row trail, the same 0.4 scanline the receipt draws. A few cells blink for three ticks.
Interaction
None. Rows invert on hover and focus, like every selection in the app.
Cost
The lowest. One pattern fill and one lit row per step.
Risk
Safe to the point of forgettable. It looks like the app, which is both the point and the weakness.

BPortal

What it says
What the bridge does, without a word: amounts ride the Ethereum lane in the clear, cross the portal, and come out the other side as static.
Background
Two lanes in a band under the hero. Items move 8 px per 70 ms step; the veil sweeps left to right as each one crosses, then it takes a stepped staircase down to the Aztec lane.
Static
Used for what it means and nothing else: the amount others cannot read. The receipt shows the other half, what you see.
Cost
Low. One noise strip per step, shared by every veiled item.
Risk
It is the bridge's landing, not the lab's. Once there are three experiments, the hero tells one story and hides the rest.

CDither tide

What it says
This is Unleashed, before you read a word. Sparse dither on the left resolves into a solid signal band on the right, the way the mark's last two columns do.
Background
Bayer 4×4 ordered dither over a slow field on the 4 px grid, one step every 150 ms. On desktop the pointer adds density that steps back down.
Interaction
A pointer trace. Nothing to learn, nothing to miss on a phone.
Cost
Moderate: about 120,000 cells per step at full width, computed at cell size and scaled up.
Risk
Atmosphere, not argument. It says nothing about privacy on its own; the copy has to.

DRelay

What it says
Unstoppable, shown instead of claimed. A message crosses a mesh; take nodes down and it reroutes from wherever it is.
Background
A stepped mesh beside the copy. The packet moves 8 px per 60 ms step along the shortest live path. Downed nodes come back after about four seconds.
Interaction
Tap a node to take it down. Two buttons give keyboard and screen-reader users the same control.
Cost
Low.
Risk
Nodes and edges is the stock crypto picture. It is a metaphor for censorship resistance, and a careful reader may take it for a diagram of Aztec's network.

EAutomaton

What it says
unleashed.systems, taken literally: simple rules, left running, never stay dead.
Background
Conway's Life on the 8 px grid, seeded with the 8×8 mark, one generation every 130 ms. Newborn cells flash signal. When the population thins out, it reseeds itself.
Interaction
Drag on the field to draw cells; tap on a phone to drop a glider.
Cost
Low to moderate.
Risk
The busiest field of the five, so every block of text needs a solid panel. Life is also a well-known screensaver.

What all five keep

  • %%I24:check%%The same content. Lockup, one headline, the lab line, Bridge and Faucet, an open slot for the next experiment, the caveat, the source.
  • %%I24:check%%Stepped motion. Every engine ticks at 7 to 17 steps a second, never eases, and stops when the tab is hidden or scrolled away.
  • %%I24:check%%A way to stop it. The motion runs longer than five seconds, so each landing ships a Pause motion control. Reduced motion shows one still frame.
  • %%I24:check%%Readable text. Copy sits on solid fills wherever a field runs behind it, so contrast stays the tokens' own.
  • %%I24:check%%Static means encrypted. Only B uses it, and only as the veil over an amount.

My pick · moderate confidence

C for the page, with a small engine per experiment.

Dither tide is the only background grown from the brand's own mark, so the page reads as Unleashed with no copy at all, and it favours no single experiment. That matters more each time the lab adds one. Give each experiment its own small engine on its card instead: B's lanes become the Bridge card, a drip for the Faucet. The page stays quiet and each experiment gets its own moment.

The strongest case against: D is the only option that proves the claim instead of making it. If the landing's job is to make "unstoppable" felt in ten seconds, D wins, at the price of a node graph that every crypto site already draws.
